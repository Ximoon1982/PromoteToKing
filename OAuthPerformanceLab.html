Scenario comparison

Warm-up: up to 15 s, at most 16 outstanding requests. Four comparison scenarios admit for 12–25 s each, adjusted to preserve the overall five-minute budget. The maximum outstanding workload is 32 requests across all POSTs; lower learned rates reduce it further. Each admitted POST has a 45 s deadline. During drain no new POSTs are launched; the next scenario starts only after all prior POSTs return. A genuine timeout stops the suite without overlapping scenarios. Isolated 429s pause admission, drain pending POSTs and honor cooldown. A second distinct episode or eight actual 429s ends admission and drains safely. Deferred requests were never launched and are not counted as API responses.

Overall rates include drain. Admission rates count only responses delivered before admission closes; use both rates when comparing scenarios with different actual durations. POST latency is the browser-observed batch wall time. Gateway latency is the reported server multiGet time; acquisition includes launch scheduling and cURL, cache ingestion is measured separately, and coordinator call time is included in acquisition (not just lock wait). Outside-gateway latency is the nonnegative difference, including transport, PHP startup or queuing and serialization, not a direct queue measurement. Dual POST repeat detects controller drift. Workload uses the profile → stats + games sequence with fair endpoint rotation and normal server canonical cache ingestion; it omits Green checks, browser cache lookups, eligibility, retries and checkpoints, so this is a transport comparison rather than an exact Recruitment reproduction. Incomplete or throttled scenarios are not ranked. The observed rate is the server’s sustained cURL-admission estimate, not the configured target or proof of the upstream limit.
